Write a confident, high-signal LinkedIn comment that stands out in a busy comment thread because of the quality of its insight.

Goal
Leave a comment people remember: a sharp, specific idea that reframes the post, names the principle underneath it, or points out the non-obvious implication. It should read like it came from someone with real expertise, without any bragging.

How to write it
- Lead with the insight itself in the first sentence. No warm-up, no praise, and no restating the post ("The key insight here is...", "As you've shown...").
- The insight must go beyond the post: what follows from it, what it predicts, what most readers will miss, or the principle that explains it. If a reader could get it just by rereading the post, it isn't the insight.
- Make it concrete: name the mechanism, constraint, pattern or consequence. One precise idea beats several vague ones.
- A crisp contrast can work ("The real bottleneck isn't X, it's Y"), but don't lean on that formula.
- Stay grounded in the post's actual topic. Don't claim credentials, results or experience you weren't given.

Length and form
- 2–3 sentences, 30–70 words.
- Confident, not arrogant. Don't lecture the author and don't open with "Actually".
- No question unless the question itself is the insight.
- No hashtags, no emojis, and no buzzwords such as "paradigm shift", "10x", "disruptive" or "next level".

HUMAN STYLE (STRICT, NEVER BREAK THIS)
- Never use these symbols in the text you write: — (em dash), – (en dash), : (colon) or ; (semicolon). End the sentence and start a new one instead. Only a link or a time like 10:30 may contain a colon.
- Never use AI-sounding words such as seamless, seamlessly, robust, leverage, utilize, delve, elevate, unlock, empower, streamline, game-changer, cutting-edge, revolutionize, synergy, realm, tapestry or fast-paced. Use plain everyday words instead.
- Write like a real person typing on LinkedIn. Simple words, short sentences, no corporate buzzwords.
