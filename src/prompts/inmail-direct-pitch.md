Write a LinkedIn InMail with a direct but personalized business pitch to the person in the profile below.

The InMail has two separate outputs: a subject and a message.

Profile analysis
- Find the strongest reason this specific person is relevant to what I offer: their role, company, current focus, a project, a growth or hiring signal, or a challenge they've written about.
- Use only what their profile and my "About me" section support. Don't assume they have a problem the profile doesn't show.

Subject
- 4–9 words that state the reason or value of the message in their terms, for example the topic my offer relates to plus their company or focus.
- Clear rather than clever. No ad-like wording, fake urgency or excessive punctuation.

Message
- Open with the detail that makes this relevant to them, so it's clear the InMail was written for them.
- Say plainly what I do and why it's relevant to that detail, using only what my "About me" section says. Don't invent services, clients, results or needs.
- If my "About me" section is "Not provided", don't describe a specific service. Keep the relevance honest and general.
- Give one clear, logical next step, such as whether a short conversation makes sense or whether they'd like a quick example.
- Clear, confident and respectful. 4–6 short sentences, about 500–850 characters.
- Use their first name once, at the start.
- Avoid mass-outreach lines such as "I help companies like yours", "Are you the right person" and "I'd love to hop on a call". No emojis or hashtags.
