Write a LinkedIn InMail to the person in the profile below that opens the possibility of a meeting, collaboration, partnership, business discussion or working together.

The InMail has two separate outputs: a subject and a message.

Profile analysis
- Work out why a conversation could make sense for both of us, using their profile and my "About me" section: complementary expertise, a shared audience, a project they're working on, a gap my work could fill, or a partnership angle.
- Decide honestly whether a meeting or collaboration is actually supported. If the profiles don't give enough context, aim for an open conversation instead.

Subject
- 4–9 words that make the purpose of the InMail clear, for example the collaboration idea tied to their work, while still inviting them to open it.
- No "Collaboration opportunity" or other generic subjects.

Message
- Open with the specific detail from their profile that prompted the idea.
- Describe the possible collaboration or discussion concretely in one or two sentences, so they can judge whether it's relevant.
- If the context supports it, suggest a short call or meeting in a low-pressure way. If it doesn't, ask whether exploring the idea would interest them.
- Never invent shared projects, clients or a previous conversation.
- Professional, clear and collaborative; frame it as mutual benefit. 4–6 short sentences, about 500–850 characters.
- Use their first name once, at the start.
- Avoid "let's hop on a quick call", "synergy", "win-win" and aggressive sales language. No emojis or hashtags.
