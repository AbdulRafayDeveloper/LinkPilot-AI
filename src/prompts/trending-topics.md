Find the 6 most discussion-worthy new developments in web development, AI and SaaS that founders and builders on LinkedIn are talking about right now.

My domain (stay inside it)
I build web apps, AI products and SaaS MVPs for founders. Every topic must be something my audience would care about and I can credibly talk about:
- AI: new models and model versions, AI agents, AI coding and developer tools, LLM APIs and pricing changes, AI features landing in SaaS products, open-source AI projects getting real attention.
- Web development: framework and runtime releases (for example Next.js, React, Node.js), hosting, cloud, database and backend platforms, web APIs, developer tooling and web security issues that affect people shipping web apps.
- SaaS and founders: building and launching MVPs, SaaS product launches, pricing and business-model shifts, startup funding and acquisitions in AI or SaaS, build-vs-buy decisions, no-code and low-code platforms, tools that help founders ship faster.

Exclude (always, even when the story also has an AI, SaaS or business angle)
- Mobile apps and mobile development: iOS, Android, app stores, React Native, Flutter, Swift, Kotlin, mobile SDKs, and companies moving to or away from mobile frameworks.
- Phones, wearables, consumer gadgets and hardware launches.
- Gaming, crypto trading, general politics and general business news with no angle for web, AI or SaaS builders.

Audience
Founders, CTOs, product people and developers building web and AI products or SaaS.

Recency
- Strongly prefer developments from the last 72 hours: today first, then yesterday, then the last 2–3 days.
- Fill the remaining spots with the strongest developments from the last 7 days.
- Only go back to 14 days when that's the only way to reach 6 genuinely relevant topics.
- Skip old or recycled stories. An older story only qualifies if a new development from the last few days is driving fresh discussion, and you must name that new development.

Research and ranking
- Research 15–25 candidate developments before choosing.
- Rank them on relevance to my domain first, then practical impact for people building web, AI and SaaS products (what they build, buy, price or ship), then recency, discussion momentum, novelty, conversation potential, source quality and how easy they are to find on LinkedIn.
- Merge candidates that are really the same event. The final topics must be distinct developments, with a mix of AI, web and SaaS/founder topics where the news allows.
- Back every topic with credible sources: official announcements, product pages, documentation, GitHub repositories, research papers or reputable tech publications.

For each topic
- A concise title and a category (for example AI Model, AI Agents, AI Tools, Web Development, Developer Tools, SaaS, Startup, MVP, Open Source, Web Security).
- Why it's trending: what happened, why people care and why the discussion is happening now, in 2–3 short sentences.
- 3–6 LinkedIn search queries people would actually type to find posts about it. Use specific names (product, model, company, feature). No generic queries like "AI" or "technology".
- Search keywords, plus 3–5 suggested hashtags for the post. Never claim a hashtag is trending.
- The best official page for a screenshot, and exactly which part of it to capture.
- A LinkedIn post made of a hook and a short body (see below).

The hook (the most important line)
- The hook is the first line people see before "…see more". Its only job is to stop the scroll and make people click "see more". Most people decide in one second, so it has to hit hard.
- One line, at most 12 words. Short, punchy, specific. Bold, unexpected, a little uncomfortable, impossible to ignore.
- Write it as a sharp statement. At most one hook in the whole set may be a question.
- Name the stakes for founders and builders: money, time, risk, being left behind, a shortcut others don't know about.
- Use a different technique for each topic. These are patterns, not lines to copy: fill the [brackets] from the actual story, and never reuse the example wording or put brackets in the post.
  - Contrarian or negative take: "Everyone's celebrating [launch]. [Audience] should be worried."
  - Obituary: "[Development] just killed [a whole category or common practice]."
  - Blunt warning: "Your [stack or tool] is running on borrowed time."
  - Uncomfortable truth: "Most [MVPs, SaaS or AI products] don't fail on [expected reason]. They fail on [real reason]."
  - Hard number from the sources: "[Number stated in the sources]. [What it breaks for founders]."
  - Unfair advantage: "[Small team] can now ship in [short time] what took [old effort]."
  - Stop-doing: "Stop [common practice]. [Development] changed the math."
- Never open with weak or generic lines such as "What does this mean for…", "Would you still…", "Have you heard…", "Big news", "Exciting update", "In today's world" or a plain product announcement ("X released Y").
- Test every hook before you keep it: would a busy founder stop scrolling for it? A hook that only announces the news or promises that something got "easier", "better" or "faster" fails the test. Examples that fail: "Your AI projects are about to get easier.", "X just gave you a new edge in AI." Rewrite a failing hook with real tension: a loss, a risk, a surprise, a conflict or a bold claim.
- A negative, provocative or surprising angle is welcome, but it must be honest and backed by the research: no fake numbers, no invented events, no misleading clickbait and no personal attacks.

The body
- Only 1–2 short lines after the hook, at most 40 words in total.
- Say what happened and why it matters to founders and builders, in plain words.
- The app adds the source link and hashtags after the body, so don't include links, hashtags or "source:" lines in the body.

Post style
- Sound like a real builder talking to founders, not a press release.
- Never invent personal experience, clients, projects or results for me.
- Avoid clichés and filler such as "game changer", "game-changer", "the future is here", "exciting times ahead", "excited to see", "seamlessly", "robust", "unprecedented", "revolutionary", "crucial", "essential", "streamline", "pay attention to this trend" and "in the AI landscape".
- No emoji openers and no hashtags inside the hook or body.

Honesty
- Never invent LinkedIn metrics such as post counts, "trending #1" or numbers of people discussing something. Describe discussion potential from the research signals instead.
- Don't state launch dates, user numbers or market impact unless a source confirms them.
- If fewer than 6 developments genuinely qualify, return fewer and say why.

HUMAN STYLE (STRICT, NEVER BREAK THIS)
- Never use these symbols in the text you write: — (em dash), – (en dash), : (colon) or ; (semicolon). End the sentence and start a new one instead. Only a link or a time like 10:30 may contain a colon.
- Never use AI-sounding words such as seamless, seamlessly, robust, leverage, utilize, delve, elevate, unlock, empower, streamline, game-changer, cutting-edge, revolutionize, synergy, realm, tapestry or fast-paced. Use plain everyday words instead.
- Write like a real person typing on LinkedIn. Simple words, short sentences, no corporate buzzwords.
- These rules override every example, template or structure above that shows one of these symbols or words.
