Find the 3 most discussion-worthy new developments in AI, software and technology that professionals on LinkedIn are talking about right now.

Focus
- New AI models and model versions, major AI product launches, new developer tools, framework and software releases, new APIs, open-source projects getting real attention, platform updates, research breakthroughs, and major acquisitions, funding rounds, partnerships or security developments.
- The audience is tech professionals on LinkedIn: developers, engineers, founders, product people and IT leaders.

Recency
- Strongly prefer developments from the last 72 hours: today first, then yesterday, then the last 2–3 days.
- Only go back as far as 7 days when nothing newer qualifies.
- Skip old or recycled stories. An older story only qualifies if a new development from the last few days is driving fresh discussion, and you must name that new development.

Research and ranking
- Research 8–15 candidate developments before choosing.
- Rank them on recency, discussion momentum, relevance to tech professionals, novelty, conversation potential, source quality and how easy they are to find on LinkedIn.
- Merge candidates that are really the same event. The final topics must be distinct developments.
- Back every topic with credible sources: official announcements, product pages, documentation, GitHub repositories, research papers or reputable tech publications.

For each topic
- A concise title and a category (for example AI Model, AI Product, Developer Tools, SaaS, Cloud, Open Source, Cybersecurity, Startup, Research).
- Why it's trending: what happened, why people care and why the discussion is happening now, in 2–3 short sentences.
- 3–6 LinkedIn search queries people would actually type to find posts about it. Use specific names (product, model, company, feature). No generic queries like "AI" or "technology".
- Search keywords, plus a few suggested hashtags. Never claim a hashtag is trending.
- A conversation angle: a specific question or debate the user can raise in comments.
- The best official page for a screenshot, and exactly which part of it to capture.
- A short LinkedIn post of 15–30 words.

Post style
- Sound like a real person thinking out loud, not a press release. Conversational, current and specific.
- Give each post a different approach:
  - Curiosity-led, e.g. "Interesting move from X. The part I'm watching most closely is…"
  - Technical observation, e.g. "This release caught my attention because…"
  - Opinion or discussion starter, e.g. "I think this could change how developers…"
- Vary sentence structure, opening, tone and technical depth naturally. Only one post should end with a question.
- Avoid clichés such as "game changer", "the future is here", "exciting times ahead", "excited to see", "seamlessly", "robust", "unprecedented" and "revolutionary".
- Don't open posts with "[Company]'s [product] is here" or a press-release style summary.
- No hashtag stuffing and no emoji openers.

Honesty
- Never invent LinkedIn metrics such as post counts, "trending #1" or numbers of people discussing something. Describe discussion potential from the research signals instead.
- Don't state launch dates, user numbers or market impact unless a source confirms them.
- If fewer than 3 developments genuinely qualify, return fewer and say why.
