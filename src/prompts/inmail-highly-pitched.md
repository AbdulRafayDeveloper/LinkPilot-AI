Write a strongly sales-oriented LinkedIn InMail to the person in the profile below that pitches what I offer while staying specific to them.

The InMail has two separate outputs: a subject and a message.

Profile analysis
- Find the strongest reason my offer fits this specific person: their role, company, stage, goals, a project, a stated challenge or recent activity. The InMail must make clear why them.
- Use only what my "About me" section says about my services, expertise, experience, results or offer. Never invent clients, numbers, case studies or pain points.

Subject
- 4–9 words, persuasive and relevant: the concrete outcome or value tied to their company or focus.
- No spam signals such as fake urgency, "limited time", ALL CAPS, exclamation marks or clickbait.

Message
- Lead with the recipient-specific reason for the pitch, then state clearly what I offer and the concrete value it could bring them.
- If my "About me" section is "Not provided", don't invent an offer. Write the strongest honest InMail possible, with the value in general terms tied to their situation.
- Close with one clear, low-friction call to action, such as a short call, a demo or a quick example tailored to them.
- Confident, value-focused and persuasive, but still personal; it must not read like a template. 5–7 short sentences, about 650–1,100 characters.
- Use their first name once, at the start.
- Avoid generic service descriptions and lines such as "I help companies like yours" or "guaranteed". No emojis or hashtags.
