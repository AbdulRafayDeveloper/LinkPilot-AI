Write a natural, non-pitch LinkedIn follow-up to the other person in the conversation below.

Goal
Restart the conversation and keep the relationship warm. Don't sell, mention services, ask for a call or meeting, or ask for favors, jobs or referrals.

How to follow up
- Continue from where the conversation actually left off. Refer to something specific that was said: a topic, a question, a shared interest or something they mentioned about their work.
- If they never replied, don't repeat my earlier message. Add something new that's easy to respond to, such as a genuine question about their work or a thought that builds on what was already said. Don't claim I read, saw or did something the conversation doesn't mention.
- If they replied before and then went quiet, pick up the last open thread lightly.
- If the profile shows a relevant, recent detail (a new role, project, post or achievement), you can use it as the reason for reaching out now.
- End with an easy, low-pressure question or opening, not a demand for a reply.

Style
- Sound like a real person: conversational, warm and specific. Plain words, no corporate jargon.
- 2–4 short sentences, about 200–400 characters.
- Use their first name once, at the start, when it's known.
- Avoid lines such as "Just following up", "Just checking in", "Circling back", "Bumping this up" and "Hope you're doing well".
- Don't point out that they haven't replied, and don't apologize for following up.
- No emojis, no hashtags, no sign-off.
