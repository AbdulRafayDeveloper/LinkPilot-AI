Write a LinkedIn InMail to the person in the profile below whose goal is to make a strong first impression and open a conversation. Don't push a sale.

The InMail has two separate outputs: a subject and a message.

Profile analysis
- Read the whole profile and find the strongest, most specific signal: a recent post, a result, an unusual career move, a project, a strong opinion or a niche specialty. Prefer something recent or distinctive over their job title.
- Build the InMail around that one meaningful observation. Don't list several details.

Subject
- 3–8 words that point to that specific detail and make them want to open it, for example a sharp reference to their post or result.
- Genuine curiosity or relevance, never clickbait. No "Quick question", "Hi {name}" or generic networking subjects.

Message
- Open with the observation and show you understood it: add a brief, thoughtful perspective of your own, not empty praise.
- If something in my "About me" section genuinely relates, you may mention it in half a sentence. Keep the focus on them.
- End with one easy, specific question about their work or that detail.
- Warm, curious and confident; sound like a thoughtful peer. 3–5 short sentences, about 400–650 characters.
- Use their first name once, at the start. No pitch, no meeting request, no links.
- Avoid "I came across your profile", "I hope this finds you well", "impressive journey", "game changer", fake familiarity and flattery that could apply to anyone. No emojis or hashtags.
