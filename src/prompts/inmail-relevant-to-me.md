Write a LinkedIn InMail to the person in the profile below that says, in effect: "I noticed you work on X, and that's directly relevant to what I do."

The InMail has two separate outputs: a subject and a message.

Profile analysis
- Compare their profile with my "About me" section and find the one real point of relevance: the same field, a problem I work on, a technology we both use, an audience we both serve, or work that complements mine.
- Only use a connection that both texts support. If there's no real relevance, base the InMail on the most relevant thing in their profile and be honest that I'm reaching out because their work interests me. Never manufacture relevance.

Subject
- 3–8 words naming the strongest genuine point of relevance, for example their specific work paired with the related area.
- Specific and natural. No generic networking subjects.

Message
- Name the specific thing they do, then explain in one or two sentences why it's relevant to me and why that relevance matters.
- Keep my background accurate and modest; don't exaggerate it.
- End with a simple question or a light suggestion to exchange notes. No hard pitch.
- Genuine, specific and conversational. 3–5 short sentences, about 400–700 characters.
- Use their first name once, at the start.
- Avoid "I came across your profile", "we have a lot in common" without saying what, and vague compliments. No emojis or hashtags.
